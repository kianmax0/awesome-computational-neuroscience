# Research topic guides

[Collection overview](../README.md) · [Start here](../START_HERE.md) · [Learn](../learn/README.md) · [Methods](../methods/README.md) · [Resource library](../resources/README.md)

These guides are entry points into questions in computational neuroscience. Each starts from a scientific problem, connects candidate models to kinds of evidence, and suggests a small exercise that can make assumptions visible. They are routes through the repository, not a claim that the field has one settled taxonomy or that a listed model is a biological mechanism.

Start with the question that interests you, then follow the linked resource and methods pages. Resource links point to the canonical entries in the resource library; use those entries for access details, descriptions, and caveats.

## How to use a guide

Treat each exercise as a proposed project that has not been run or validated by this repository. Begin with a defined observable, a model with explicit assumptions, and one comparison that could distinguish explanations. Report what the analysis can establish and what remains unidentifiable from the available data. When a guide names a benchmark, database, model, or toolbox, follow its resource entry for current access conditions and implementation details.

## Coverage and connections

The current guides emphasize cellular and circuit models, neural coding and learning, inference, cognition and action, and comparisons with artificial systems. They connect to neural-data methods across recording modalities. Additional guides cover connectomics, neural development, computational psychiatry, and neuromorphic systems. Their scopes differ: they introduce particular questions and resource routes rather than exhaust these fields. Molecular and cell-type modeling, glial computation, detailed device physics, and many clinical or species-specific directions remain outside dedicated coverage.

A resource can serve several questions. Follow neighboring-topic links rather than treating these pages as mutually exclusive branches, and use [Contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md) to suggest a guide that fills a clear gap.
