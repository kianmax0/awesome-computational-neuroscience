Computational neuroscience connects biological measurements and questions with mathematical descriptions, models, and computational analysis. You can start by understanding an idea, inspecting a figure or dataset, deriving a result, or writing code. Programming is useful for many projects, but it is not an entry requirement for learning the concepts.

## Start from a question or a background

If you are curious about what neurons compute, begin with [neurons and dendrites](../topics/neurons-and-dendrites.md) or [coding and representations](../topics/coding-and-representations.md). If you want to understand population activity, look at [circuits and dynamics](../topics/circuits-and-dynamics.md). Questions about learning, uncertainty, behavior, memory, or movement have routes through [learning and plasticity](../topics/learning-and-plasticity.md), [inference and prediction](../topics/inference-and-prediction.md), [perception and decision](../topics/perception-and-decision.md), [memory and navigation](../topics/memory-and-navigation.md), and [action and control](../topics/action-and-control.md). [NeuroAI](../topics/neuroai.md) connects questions about brains with artificial systems.

Choose a prerequisite only when a resource or question needs it. Biology knowledge helps you interpret what a model represents; mathematics helps you follow its assumptions and behavior; programming lets you implement and inspect computational predictions; familiarity with experimental measurement helps connect models to data. Strength in one area does not imply a particular level in the others.

## Learn at different levels in parallel

The guides describe capabilities and next actions, not credentials or a single ladder. A learner can read theory without coding, start programming while learning biology, or analyze recordings while building statistics. Use [prerequisite self-checks](prerequisites.md) to identify a specific gap, then return to a [learning path](learning-paths.md), [topic](../topics/README.md), or [method](../methods/README.md) that uses it.

Exercises and projects in this guide are suggestions and have not been run by the repository. Check the linked resource's own documentation, version, data terms, and requirements before using it in a project.

## Extend a route into a research direction

| Interest | Continue with | A bounded next outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wiring, morphology, and growth | [Connectomics](../topics/connectomics.md) and [development](../topics/development-and-self-organization.md) | Define a graph's nodes and edges, or compare two explicit growth rules. |
| Individual differences in behavior and brain measurements | [Computational psychiatry](../topics/computational-psychiatry.md) | Distinguish a reference-distribution deviation, a fitted cognitive parameter, and a validated prediction. |
| Computing with events or neural hardware | [Neuromorphic computing](../topics/neuromorphic-computing.md) | Separate the model, its software execution, its hardware mapping, and the measurements needed for an efficiency claim. |
| Inverting a mechanistic simulator | [Simulation-based inference](../methods/simulation-based-inference.md) | Recover known parameters and inspect uncertainty and predictive checks. |
| Explaining an intervention's effect | [Causal inference and design](../methods/causal-inference-and-experimental-design.md) | Define the target effect, experimental unit, and assumptions supporting a comparison. |
