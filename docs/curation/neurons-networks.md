# Curation research: neuron models and network dynamics

## Scope and method

I read the full `README.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`. The repository already has broad simulator coverage, several introductory books and courses, a general ModelDB entry, Allen Brain Observatory resources, and the *Neuronal Dynamics* book plus its Python exercises (one educational resource, now represented together). I therefore screened for missing resource types and usable scientific content rather than additional general simulators or many individual papers. Software candidates were checked for working official documentation and signs of an active, non-archived project. A page loading successfully was not treated as evidence of scientific value.

## Discovery hubs visited

- [Open Computational Neuroscience Resources](https://github.com/asoplata/open-computational-neuroscience-resources) — broad directory of training, simulation, model repositories, and standards; used as a discovery index, not as verification of its linked items.
- [Awesome Computational Neuroscience (sakimarquis)](https://github.com/sakimarquis/awesome-computational-neuroscience) — course, package, talk, summer-school, and book directory; used to identify university and INCF leads.
- [Computational Neuroscience Resources (Dan Goodman)](https://neural-reckoning.org/comp-neuro-resources.html) — books, courses, specialized tutorials, and links to OSB and other lists.
- [INCF TrainingSpace](https://training.incf.org/) — searched and opened course-level records for neuron-model, network-dynamics, and large-scale modeling instruction.
- [ModelDB](https://modeldb.science/) — browsed official model records and the curated network-model index.
- [Open Source Brain](https://v1.opensourcebrain.org/) — inspected the legacy OSB project directory and its notice pointing users to OSB v2; also inspected a Purkinje model record.
- [Neural Latents Benchmark](https://neurallatents.github.io/) — followed its related-benchmarks link to investigate the Computation-through-Dynamics benchmark and its official toolkit.

### Ready-to-insert entries

- [Allen Cell Types Database: Neuronal Models](https://brain-map.org/support/documentation/cell-types-database-api) - Browse electrophysiology-linked GLIF point-neuron and perisomatic biophysical models with dendritic morphology, then download and run models through the Allen SDK; biophysical models require NEURON.
- [Dendrify](https://dendrify.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) - Build reduced-compartment neuron models with active dendritic and synaptic properties for Brian 2 networks; install with Python and Brian 2, and use the official tutorials for custom models and simulation.
- [Jaxley](https://jaxley.readthedocs.io/en/stable/) - Define and fit differentiable multicompartment biophysical neuron models with JAX on CPU, GPU, or TPU; CPU support is available through the standard install, while accelerators require a compatible JAX backend.
- [Neuroscience for Machine Learners (Neuro4ML)](https://training.incf.org/course/neuroscience-machine-learners-neuro4ml) - A free INCF course for machine-learning audiences covering biophysical and multicompartment neuron models, synapses, Dendrify, networks, and accompanying practical exercises.

### Thin cells

The verified set has good coverage for point-neuron, AdEx, plasticity, and reduced or detailed multicompartment modeling. There is thinner coverage for dendritic model fitting against experimental traces, standardized comparison across dendrite-capable frameworks, and maintained, fully validated open implementations of canonical detailed dendritic cells. The OSB Purkinje record is not a substitute because its own status notes say the conversion is incomplete.

### Ready-to-insert entries - network dynamics

- [Modeling Neural Circuits Made Simple with Python](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262548083/modeling-neural-circuits-made-simple-with-python/) - An accessible computational neuroscience text that builds from neuron biophysics to neural coding, learning, and whole-network models, with Python notebooks and an open-access edition; the publisher lists a CC BY-NC-ND license, and print/e-book editions are also available.
- [Neural Fields: Theory and Applications](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-54593-1) - An edited reference on neural-field models, spatially extended population dynamics, and their mathematical foundations; full-book access may require a purchase or library subscription.
- [Dynamical Neural Systems](https://training.incf.org/course/dynamical-neural-systems) - A free INCF course covering neural stability, oscillations, bursting, coupled oscillators, firing-rate networks, and spatiotemporal patterns through a structured lecture sequence.
- [Large-Scale Modeling of Brain Dynamics](https://training.incf.org/course/large-scale-modeling-brain-dynamics) - A hands-on INCF tutorial implementing coupled Wilson–Cowan neural masses with empirical structural connectivity to explore BOLD signals and lesion effects; course access is linked through Learn Gala.
- [Cajal Course in Computational Neuroscience](https://training.incf.org/course/cajal-course-computational-neuroscience) - A structured graduate-level course with focused units on balanced excitatory–inhibitory network stability, gain modulation, and rate-based and spiking balanced-network dynamics; free online materials.
- [Computation-through-Dynamics Benchmark and Toolkit](https://github.com/snel-repo/ComputationThroughDynamicsToolkit) - Compare inferred neural dynamics against task-trained reference models using synthetic spiking datasets and interpretable metrics; installation uses Conda/Python 3.10 and Git LFS, the walkthrough assets require several gigabytes of disk, and GPU access may be needed for default data-model training.
- [Potjans–Diesmann Cortical Microcircuit Model in NetPyNE](https://modeldb.science/266872) - A reproducible layered cortical network implementation with scripts for rescaling and extending the model to multicompartment neurons, available through ModelDB and its linked code repository; requires Python, NetPyNE, and NEURON, and full-size runs can be compute-intensive.

### Thin cells - network dynamics

Coverage is now stronger for rate-based population models, neural-field theory, whole-brain/connectome modeling, one reproducible spiking microcircuit, and a task-trained neural-dynamics benchmark. Candidate space remains thin for benchmarks on biological neural data with standardized network-dynamics outputs, teaching material that directly compares rate-based and spiking models on the same task, and maintained open network-model collections with explicit validation targets. The NEST and NetPyNE Potjans examples are two implementations of one base model, so only one was selected.

## Unresolved verification and caveats

- The University of Missouri hands-on course page could not be opened by the browser tool; its promising notebook sequence remains a lead, not a verified recommendation.
- The stable NEST URL for the Potjans example returned 404. The versioned NEST v3.7 example page remains readable, but ModelDB already provides a distinct NetPyNE implementation of the same reference model, so the NEST page is deferred.
- The INCF `Module 3: Computational Models` record describes a self-contained AdEx tutorial but does not identify its parent curriculum. The separately verified `Introduction to computational neuroscience` study track lists 24 lectures and six courses but does not list this module among those courses; the module is deferred until its curriculum relationship is established.
- The older Open Source Brain directory points readers to OSB v2. The specific legacy Purkinje implementation reviewed here reports incomplete conversion and does not meet the ready-to-use bar.
- Several INCF entries are courses containing multiple lessons. Recommendations above link once to each official parent course and describe its outline; individual lesson pages are not treated as separate resources.

Final integration uses the verified [author-maintained book PDF and notebooks](https://github.com/RobertRosenbaum/ModelingNeuralCircuits) as the direct entry for Modeling Neural Circuits Made Simple with Python. The INCF large-scale tutorial is retained with an explicit interactive-access verification caveat.
