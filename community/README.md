# Community, training, and help

[Collection overview](../README.md) · [Learn](../learn/README.md) · [Topics](../topics/README.md) · [Resource library](../resources/README.md)

Use communities to hear how questions are framed, find feedback, and learn the practices behind published work. The [community catalog](../resources/community.md) holds the official organization and forum links; the guidance below helps choose a starting point.

## Find a place to participate

| What you need | Where to start | A useful first action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hear current systems-neuroscience research | COSYNE in the [community catalog](../resources/community.md). | Read abstracts around one topic and list the models and measurements being compared. |
| Find computational-neuroscience meetings and education | Organization for Computational Neurosciences in the [catalog](../resources/community.md). | Look for official meeting, workshop, or educational information relevant to your question. |
| Explore research networks and training opportunities | Bernstein Network in the [catalog](../resources/community.md). | Check current research groups, events, and training information on the official site. |
| Find training and standards communities | INCF in the [catalog](../resources/community.md), plus [courses](../resources/courses-and-lectures.md). | Choose a lesson or training series that addresses a specific background gap. |
| Ask about software, neuroimaging, or data workflows | Neurostars in the [catalog](../resources/community.md). | Search previous answers, then write a reproducible question if the issue remains. |

Event schedules, eligibility, fees, and application dates change. Follow the official resource to check those details before planning participation; this guide does not maintain a separate calendar.

## Ask a question others can answer

State the scientific or technical goal first. For code or data problems, include relevant software versions, a small example, the expected result, the observed result, and the exact error if one occurred. Share public or permitted example data rather than identifying participant information or restricted recordings.

For a theoretical question, define the quantities and assumptions, show the step you are unsure about, and explain what would settle the question. For a paper discussion, distinguish the authors' claim, the evidence shown, and your interpretation.

## Start a reading group or class activity

Choose one question and pair an accessible introduction with one original paper, model, or dataset. Ask participants to identify assumptions, reconstruct one argument or figure, and compare an alternative explanation. Rotate the roles of presenter, methods reader, and evidence critic.

The [teaching guide](../learn/teaching.md) provides session formats and learning outcomes. [Projects](../learn/projects.md) offer small exercises, and [reproducible research](../methods/reproducible-research.md) explains how to make the resulting work inspectable.

## Contribute back

An accurate broken-link report, a clarified prerequisite, or a useful comparison can help the next reader. Follow [Contributing](../CONTRIBUTING.md), identify the affected catalog entry or guide, and state what you checked. Keep resource recommendations connected to an explicit learning or research need.

For structured research training, see [MBL Methods in Computational Neuroscience](../resources/courses-and-lectures.md#mbl-methods-in-computational-neuroscience) and the [Computational Psychiatry Course](../resources/courses-and-lectures.md#computational-psychiatry-course). Their catalog entries distinguish course scope from participation and access conditions.
