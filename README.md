# Awesome Computational Neuroscience [![Awesome](https://awesome.re/badge.svg)](https://awesome.re)

> Resources for understanding brains through mathematical models, neural computation, and data.

A curated guide for curious readers, learners, researchers, and teachers. Explore a scientific question, build the background you need, or find materials for a specific task. You can begin with the conceptual overview without writing code.

## Start here

| What would you like to do? | Go to | What you will find |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Understand the field | [Start here](START_HERE.md) | Concrete questions, examples of models and measurements, and a map of the collection. |
| Learn systematically | [Learn](learn/README.md) | Background bridges, ordered learning paths, small projects, and teaching suggestions. |
| Explore a research question | [Topics](topics/README.md) | Guides connecting scientific questions to theories, experiments, models, and data. |
| Build, derive, analyze, or evaluate | [Methods and workflows](methods/README.md) | Practical routes through theoretical modeling, simulation, neural data, fitting, comparison, and reproduction. |
| Find a particular kind of material | [Resource library](resources/README.md) | Books, courses, tutorials, selected papers, software, datasets, benchmarks, and standards. |
| Meet people or get help | [Community](community/README.md) | Research networks, meetings, training, and technical discussion. |

If you already know what you need, go directly to the relevant guide or catalog. The learning routes are optional; you do not need to complete a general curriculum before exploring a topic.

## How to use this collection

- Choose a question or a small outcome first. A guide explains why a resource is useful at that point; the library holds its full entry.
- Match prerequisites to individual skills. Experience in mathematics, biology, programming, and experimental work can differ; there is no single level assigned to a person.
- Each resource has one catalog entry. Topic pages, learning paths, and workflows link to it rather than maintain separate copies.
- Access notes distinguish free materials, publisher listings, accounts, and data-use agreements. Linked resources retain their own licenses and terms.
- Projects are suggested exercises. Reading documentation, checking a link, running code, and reproducing a scientific result are different checks; the repository does not treat one as proof of another.

The collection concentrates on computational accounts of neural activity, learning, behavior, and cognition, together with the methods needed to study them. The [topic map](topics/README.md) states its coverage and boundaries. Selection favors an identifiable author or project, useful documentation, and a clear learning or research purpose.

## Contributing

Suggest a resource or report a broken link through [Issues](https://github.com/kianmax0/awesome-computational-neuroscience/issues), or follow [Contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md) to send a focused pull request. Explain what the resource helps someone understand or do, place its entry in one library page, and link it from the guides where it adds value.

See [Maintaining](MAINTAINING.md) for formatting, file and anchor checks, external-link checks, and ongoing editorial review. [Curation records](docs/curation/README.md) preserve past decisions and the limits of earlier checks.
